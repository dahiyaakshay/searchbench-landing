From a CSV to a published page

01
Drop in the exports

Search Console pages and queries, plus GA4 if you have it. Each file is classified from its column headings, cleaned, and reported back: 345 pages, 978 queries, three breakdowns, two files tidied.

02
Something stands out

A page ranks 4.2 on 246 impressions and draws 8 clicks. The click-through rate typical for position 4.2 is about 15%, so it should be drawing closer to 37. It is at 0.21× what the position is worth.

03
The evidence is on the row

No score, no grade. The impressions, the position, the curve and the shortfall all sit beside the finding, labelled counted rather than rule of thumb, so you can verify it against the same export in a spreadsheet if you want to.

Every query mapped to that page, each marked measured or inferred. What is wrong with it, a checklist of specific instructions, and whatever the crawler read from the page itself. Free to open, and nothing in it is generated.

The queries it already earns clicks on are named in the prompt and preserved. Research and draft run as separate calls, your brand kit is applied while writing and verified afterwards by counting, and internal links are checked against pages that exist.

06
Read it, then take it

It lands in a library. A blocking issue refuses approval outright. What you approve exports as Markdown or Word, and you publish it yourself. Total spend for that page: about $0.05.

What a title-and-meta checker misses

Who you are blocking without knowing

GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot are checked separately. A site can be open to Google and closed to everything that answers questions about it.

Whether the answer is extractable

Retrieval reads passages, not pages. If the answer only emerges over three scrolls there is nothing clean to lift, however good the page is.

Whether there is anything to cite

Answers get built from specifics. A page of general advice has nothing a model can attribute, so it tends to get read and not credited.

The whole text, not the opening

The readable body is kept, so a check can look past the first paragraph and the rewrite has the page to work from instead of asking you to paste it.

Decisions

Three choices that shaped everything else

Each of these closed a door on purpose. The costs are as interesting as the benefits.

Deterministic analysis, with no model anywhere near it

Problem

A tool that asks a model to judge your pages gives a different answer on Tuesday, cannot show its working, and costs money every time you look. Nobody can audit a finding they cannot reproduce.

Decision

Every check is arithmetic: stemmed matching, shingled similarity, a click-through curve by position. No model is called to produce a finding, ever.

Outcome

Reading costs nothing and runs offline. Each finding carries the rows it came from. The cost is that it cannot make judgements a human would, so it does not pretend to.

Manual exports instead of API integrations

Problem

Connecting to Search Console, Analytics and a keyword API means OAuth, stored credentials, rate limits, a server to hold them, and three paid subscriptions before the tool does anything.

Decision

You export the CSVs you already download and drop them in. Fourteen kinds are recognised from their column headings rather than their names.

Outcome

No account, no credentials, no subscription, no data on anyone else's server. The cost is a minute of exporting and no scheduled runs, which is the right trade for a tool you own.

Spend only after a free check says it is worth it

Problem

Programmatic SEO fails by writing four hundred near-identical pages at cost, then discovering the rows were thin, duplicated, or clashing with pages that already existed.

Decision

Preflight reads every row before a single call. Thin rows, near-duplicates and address clashes are flagged for free. Budgets stop a run; a resume does not pay twice.

Outcome

The expensive mistake becomes a free one. Writing fifty pages costs a few dollars because the rows that were never worth writing got caught before they were.

The hard parts

What was difficult to get right

The problems that took the longest, and what each one actually turned out to be.

Matching queries to pages with no pairs

Search Console will give you a list of queries and a list of pages, but not which query belongs to which page unless you export per page. Stemmed lexical overlap with a confidence score handles the rest, measured pairs always win over inferred ones, and a pin lets you overrule both. The lesson was to label the difference on screen rather than blend them into one number.

Absence of evidence read as absence

An export only lists pages that earned an impression, so a page that ranks for nothing looks identical to a page that was never built. Eight separate places in the codebase made that leap independently. A test now walks every file and fails if a ninth appears.

448,500 comparisons on every screen

345 pages against 1,300 queries re-tokenised the same strings three million times, and three endpoints each rebuilt the same mapping in parallel. Caching the stemmer and building the mapping once behind a lock took the post-crawl reload from 21.7 seconds to 0.1.

Keeping generation out of the analysis

It is tempting to let a model summarise a finding or guess a mapping, and every time it would have been easier. Holding that line is what makes a finding checkable, and it is why reading costs nothing. The separation had to be structural rather than a convention, or it would have eroded.

Questions

The things worth asking

Does it connect to Search Console?

No. You export the CSVs and drop them in, which takes about a minute. That is the trade for keeping the data on your own machine.

What does it cost to run?

Reading, checking and mapping cost nothing at all, because no model is involved in any of it. Writing pages goes through your own API key, with the running total on screen and a ceiling you set.

Will it get me ranking?

It tells you where you already rank and what is costing you clicks there. No tool can honestly promise the rest, and one that does is selling you something.

What does it need installed?

Python 3.11 or newer. No database, no node_modules, no build step. Unzip it, run one command, and open the page it gives you.

Does it publish for me?

No. Everything written waits in a library until you have read it. A page with a blocking issue refuses approval outright, and what you approve exports as Markdown or Word.

Does it work in other languages?

The arithmetic does. The word matching is English only, so query-to-page mapping will be weaker elsewhere and is worth checking by hand.
